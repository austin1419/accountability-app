# Domain 1 — Nutrition Science & Behavior Change
**Experts: Dr. Layne Norton · Precision Nutrition**

---

## Overview

Nutrition is the most commonly confused domain in fitness. Clients arrive with contradictory beliefs from social media, fad diets, and decades of bad public health messaging. PULSE coaches from an evidence hierarchy that centers energy balance, protein adequacy, and sustainable behavior change — not trends.

---

### Principle: Energy Balance Is Real and Primary
Weight is regulated by total calories in vs. out. Hormones, medications, and stress affect WHERE energy goes in the equation — not whether the equation exists. Telling a client "energy balance doesn't apply to me" is never true.

### Coaching Application
Find where the equation is being disrupted. A client with genuine hormonal issues still operates under energy balance — their set point and partitioning are affected, not the law itself. The coaching job is to identify the hidden variables: undercounting, sleep disruption, extreme stress, or medication effects.

### Signals
Use when: client believes calories don't count for them, reports "eating well" but not losing weight, claims a slow metabolism.

---

### Principle: Protein — 0.7–1.0g Per Pound of Bodyweight Daily
This is the practical target for muscle retention and growth. Total daily intake matters most. Distribution across meals is secondary but improves with 30–40g per meal for adults over 40. Protein is the single highest-leverage nutrition intervention for virtually every client.

### Coaching Application
Start here before any other nutrition conversation. A client asking about meal timing who doesn't hit protein is working at the wrong tier. For 40+ adults: 35–40g per meal is the minimum to clear the leucine threshold needed for muscle protein synthesis.

### Signals
Use when: any client regardless of goal. Especially: plateau, muscle loss, fatigue, poor recovery, weight loss with no body composition improvement.

---

### Principle: Flexible Dieting Outperforms Rigid Dieting
No food is inherently good or evil. Rigid elimination rules fail because they create psychological pressure that leads to bingeing. Teaching clients to fit foods into targets is more durable than providing a banned list. Adherence is the strongest predictor of diet success — not the diet itself.

### Coaching Application
When a client wants to eliminate a food group, ask: "Does removing this make it harder to hit your protein?" If yes, that's the conversation. If a protocol works because it creates a deficit, the novelty doesn't explain the result.

### Signals
Use when: client follows strict elimination diets, reports cycles of restriction and bingeing, asks about keto/carnivore/time-restricted feeding.

---

### Principle: Sleep and Stress Are Nutritional Variables
Chronically elevated cortisol promotes fat storage and impairs recovery. Sleep deprivation increases ghrelin (hunger hormone) and suppresses leptin (satiety hormone). The poorly slept client who "can't control hunger" is experiencing a biological reality — not moral failure.

### Coaching Application
Fix sleep before blaming nutrition. A client who eats "perfectly" but sleeps 5 hours is fighting their own hormonal environment. Address sleep and stress first, then reassess eating patterns.

### Signals
Use when: client reports hunger they can't control, cravings for calorie-dense foods, inability to stick to plan despite "trying."

---

### Principle: Hierarchy Before Optimization
Fix sleep, total protein, and caloric structure before worrying about meal timing, micronutrient cycling, or advanced supplementation. A client asking about ashwagandha who doesn't hit protein is working at the wrong tier.

### Norton's Nutrition Hierarchy — Apply in Order:
1. **Energy Balance** — Total calories govern body composition direction. Non-negotiable.
2. **Macronutrients** — Protein first (0.7–1.0g/lb). Carb/fat split matters less than adherence to target.
3. **Micronutrients** — Whole foods, variety, adequate fiber. Address deficiencies before optimization.
4. **Meal Timing** — Minor influence. Pre/post-workout protein has some benefit. Most timing detail is noise.
5. **Supplements** — Creatine, protein powder, vitamin D, omega-3. Everything else is conditional at best.

### Coaching Application
When a client asks about a diet trend, evaluate it against energy balance and protein adequacy first. Does this protocol make it harder to hit protein? Does it work because it creates a deficit?

### Signals
Use when: client asks about supplements, advanced timing protocols, or trendy interventions.

---

### Principle: Creatine — Universal Recommendation
5g daily, any timing. Improves lean mass, strength, power output, cognitive performance, bone health, and has emerging evidence for mood and depression. There is no credible reason not to take it. This includes women.

### Coaching Application
Recommend universally. Do not wait for the client to ask. Address common myths (water retention, bloating) directly. Women especially under-utilize creatine due to misconceptions.

### Signals
Use when: client asks about supplements, or as a baseline recommendation for any client not currently taking creatine.

---

### Principle: Adherence Is the Variable — The Compliance Problem
The most perfect diet that a client doesn't follow produces nothing. A less-than-optimal diet followed consistently beats perfection inconsistently every time.

### Coaching Application
- Identify the smallest change that moves the needle. The client eating 60g protein needs to reach 80g before worrying about macros.
- Self-monitoring is the most consistently supported behavior change tool. Logging food — even imperfectly — increases awareness. A client who logs 5 of 7 days outperforms one who doesn't log at all.
- Perfection is the enemy of consistency. One bad meal doesn't derail a week. Not logging Sunday because Saturday was off the rails does derail the week. Log the bad days. Especially the bad days.

### Signals
Use when: client has stopped logging, gives up after one bad day, seeks a "perfect" protocol rather than a consistent one.

## Precision Nutrition Framework

### Principle: Skills → Practices → Goals Progression
Most coaches skip to goals. PN builds from skills upward.

- **Skills** — Can the client do the behavior under any condition? Skills are the foundation. Without them, practices are fragile.
- **Practices** — Regular, repeated application of a skill. Specific, bounded, achievable. Practices build automaticity.
- **Goals** — The long-term outcome. Relevant to keep motivation alive — but not the focus of daily coaching. "Lose 30 pounds" is a goal. It is NOT a practice.

### Coaching Application
When prescribing a behavior change, assign a practice — not a goal. "This week, add protein to breakfast every morning" is a practice. "Lose 5 pounds" is not a coaching instruction.

---

### Principle: The Two Master Practices
PN's research identified two practices with the highest leverage on virtually every nutrition outcome.

- **Eat slowly** — The satiety signal takes 15–20 minutes to register. Speed-eating chronically overrides it. This is not about willpower — it's physiology.
- **Eat until 80% full** — Stop before the plate is clean. This practice, combined with eating slowly, addresses overconsumption at the neurological level.

### Coaching Application
When a client is overwhelmed or early in their journey, reduce the entire nutrition conversation to these two practices and nothing else. Nothing else until they're consistent.

---

### Principle: Client Readiness — Match Intervention to Stage

| Stage | Description | Coaching Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-contemplation | Doesn't believe change is necessary | Plant seeds, raise awareness. No prescription. |
| Contemplation | Knows they need change but not ready | Explore the gap. Not a protocol. |
| Preparation | Intends to act, small steps taken | One specific, achievable starting point only. |
| Action | Actively making changes | Skill-building, accountability, obstacle-solving. |
| Maintenance | 6+ months consistent | Relapse prevention, identity consolidation. |

### Coaching Application
Before prescribing any nutrition protocol, assess where the client is. A Contemplation client doesn't need a macro plan — they need a question: "What's one small thing you could try this week?"

## Nutrition Myth Responses

| Myth | Evidence-Based Response |
|---|---|
| "Carbs make you fat" | Excess calories make you fat. Carbs in the context of an appropriate caloric target do not cause fat gain. |
| "I have a slow metabolism" | Metabolic rate differences between similar individuals are small (±200 cal/day). The most common "slow metabolism" is systematic underestimation of caloric intake. |
| "I don't eat that much" | Self-reported intake is notoriously inaccurate — studies show underreporting by 20–40%. |
| "Clean eating is enough" | Clean food still contains calories. 4,000 calories of "clean" food still creates a surplus. |
| "Fat loss requires suffering" | A 300–500 calorie daily deficit produces 0.5–1 lb/week without hormonal disruption. Sustainable deficits outperform extreme deficits in long-term results. |
