# Domain 5 — Sleep, Recovery & HRV
**Experts: Dr. Matthew Walker · Dr. Andrew Huberman**

---

## Overview

Sleep is not a lifestyle preference — it is a physiological non-negotiable. Without adequate sleep, no nutrition plan, training program, or behavior change intervention performs as designed. The AI coach treats sleep as the foundational variable that determines whether everything else works.

---

### Principle: Sleep Architecture — Why Every Stage Matters

| Stage | Function |
|---|---|
| NREM Stage 1 & 2 (Light) | Transition and consolidation. Brain begins filtering signal from noise. Memory consolidation begins. |
| NREM Stage 3 (Deep / Slow Wave) | Physical restoration. Growth hormone released in largest nocturnal pulse. Muscle repair and synthesis. Immune strengthening. Glymphatic brain cleaning. Dominates early in the night. |
| REM Sleep | Emotional processing, memory integration, creativity, problem solving. Dominates the second half of the night — the last 2 hours of an 8-hour sleep window contain most of the night's REM. Cutting sleep from 8 to 6 hours eliminates most REM, not just 25% of it. |

### Coaching Application
When a client reports cutting sleep short, the conversation isn't just about tiredness — it's about missing nearly all their REM sleep (emotional regulation, decision-making) and cutting into deep sleep (muscle repair, GH release). This is why the client who sleeps 6 hours feels functional but makes worse food decisions and skips workouts.

### Signals
Use when: client reports any sleep issues, poor recovery, muscle soreness that persists, appetite dysregulation, or brain fog.

---

### Principle: Sleep Is a Body Composition Variable
Sleep deprivation directly undermines fat loss and accelerates muscle loss.

- One study (Nedeltcheva et al., Annals of Internal Medicine) found sleep-deprived dieters lost 55% less fat and 60% more muscle than adequately sleeping dieters on the same caloric deficit.
- A single night of poor sleep elevates ghrelin (hunger) and suppresses leptin (satiety). The client who "can't control eating" the day after a bad night is responding to a real hormonal signal — not failing willpower.
- Even one week of sleep restriction (5–6 hours/night) produces insulin sensitivity comparable to pre-diabetic levels in otherwise healthy adults.

### Coaching Application
Nutrition advice without sleep advice is incomplete. When a client is struggling with hunger control, food decisions, or plateau — ask about sleep before adjusting the nutrition plan. Fix the sleep, change the hormonal environment, then reassess.

### Signals
Use when: client reports inability to control hunger, cravings for calorie-dense foods, fat loss plateau, poor recovery from training, muscle soreness that lingers.

---

### Principle: Testosterone and Growth Hormone Are Sleep-Dependent
Both are secreted primarily during sleep.

- Chronic sleep restriction reduces testosterone in men by 10–15% within one week — equivalent to aging 10–15 years.
- GH pulses are disrupted by insufficient deep sleep, impairing the muscle repair that training was designed to produce.
- The client who trains hard and sleeps poorly is essentially leaving their training results on the table.

### Coaching Application
For male clients concerned about performance, recovery, or body composition who aren't sleeping enough: address sleep before any discussion of supplementation or advanced protocols. "You're asking about creatine timing, but you told me you're sleeping 5 hours. That's the conversation."

### Signals
Use when: male client with performance concerns, poor recovery, low energy — especially before recommending any supplementation.

### Principle: Sleep Optimization Hierarchy — Apply in Order

1. **Consistent wake time** — Set a non-negotiable wake time 7 days a week. More impactful than bedtime. Start here.
2. **Morning light exposure** — 10–30 minutes of bright light (ideally outdoor) within 30–60 minutes of waking. Sets the circadian clock. Determines melatonin release timing 12–16 hours later.
3. **Evening light management** — Blue light and bright white light after sunset suppresses melatonin by up to 50%. Dim the environment after 9pm.
4. **Temperature** — Core body temperature must drop 1–3°F to initiate sleep. Cool bedroom (65–68°F optimal).
5. **Caffeine curfew** — Caffeine half-life is 5–7 hours; quarter-life is 10–12 hours. Cut caffeine by 12–1pm for most clients. Earlier for those over 45 (caffeine metabolism slows with age).
6. **Alcohol** — Even 1–2 drinks reduce REM sleep by 20–25%. "Sleeping well" after drinking is sedation, not restorative sleep.
7. **Consistent bedtime** — Once wake time is established, wind bedtime back to achieve 7–9 hours. Create a 30–60 minute low-stimulation wind-down routine.

### Coaching Application
When a client reports poor sleep, work through this hierarchy before recommending supplements or advanced interventions. Most sleep problems can be significantly improved with items 1–3 alone.

### Signals
Use when: client reports poor sleep quality, difficulty falling asleep, waking at night, fatigue despite adequate sleep hours.

### Principle: HRV as a Recovery Metric
Heart Rate Variability (HRV) measures the variation in time between heartbeats — regulated by the autonomic nervous system. Higher HRV = better recovery and readiness. Lower HRV = the nervous system is under stress.

- **What lowers HRV:** alcohol, illness, overtraining, psychological stress, poor sleep, insufficient recovery
- **What raises HRV:** adequate sleep, regular training (long-term), controlled breathing, cold exposure
- **Individual trends matter more than absolute values.** A client whose HRV drops 20% below their normal morning baseline should consider a recovery day.

### Coaching Application
When a client presents with wearable data (WHOOP, Garmin, Oura) showing low HRV: don't override it with "push through it" messaging. Ask: "What does your body seem to be telling you? What happened in the last 48 hours that might explain this?" HRV is a conversation starter — not an absolute prescriber.

### Signals
Use when: client uses a wearable, asks about training readiness, is chronically fatigued, or presents data showing recovery issues.

---

### Principle: Special Population Sleep Considerations

- **Perimenopausal and menopausal women** — Night sweats, hot flashes, and progesterone decline dramatically disrupt sleep. Sleep deprivation compounds every other symptom of the transition. This is a clinical issue — encourage conversation with a menopause specialist about hormonal management.
- **High-stress life phases** — New parents, job change, major life upheaval: reduce training intensity expectations and prioritize sleep quality as the first lever. Not the training — the sleep.
- **High-volume training athletes** — Standard 7–9 hour recommendation may be insufficient. Elite athletes performing optimally sleep 9–10 hours. Calibrate sleep targets to training load.

### Coaching Application
Match sleep targets and expectations to the client's current life context. A new mother sleeping 4 hours is not a client who needs a harder training program — she needs support and reduced expectations until sleep stabilizes.
