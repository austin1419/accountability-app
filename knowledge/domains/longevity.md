# Domain 8 — Movement, Longevity & Mobility
**Experts: Dr. Kelly Starrett · Dr. Aaron Horschig · Dr. Peter Attia**

---

## Overview

Movement quality is the foundation on which all training is built. Longevity is the long-horizon outcome every client deserves to be coached toward, even when they think they're just "trying to lose weight." This domain covers movement screening, mobility, rehab principles, and the Attia longevity framework.

---

### Principle: Position Before Load (Starrett)
Load amplifies whatever pattern it's placed on. A squat with compromised positioning loaded to 200lbs is a faster path to injury — not a training stimulus. Before adding load, establish the position.

### Coaching Application
When a client reports pain or dysfunction during a movement: remove the load first, restore the position, then reintroduce load progressively. "How does it feel with no weight?" is the first diagnostic question.

### Signals
Use when: client reports joint pain during training, has movement compensations visible in form, or is returning from injury.

---

### Principle: The Biopsychosocial Pain Model (Starrett)
Pain is not purely structural. It is influenced by:
- **Biological factors** — tissue damage, inflammation, nerve irritation
- **Psychological factors** — fear-avoidance, catastrophizing, stress
- **Social factors** — relationship to movement, identity around injury, support systems

### Coaching Application
When a client reports pain: do not immediately validate their belief that the movement is "broken" or permanently dangerous. Ask: "When does it hurt? What makes it better? Worse?" Fear-avoidance (avoiding movement because movement hurts) often prolongs pain more than the original injury.

### Signals
Use when: client has chronic pain, has been told to avoid exercise by a provider, or catastrophizes about pain during training.

---

### Principle: The Eight Foundational Movements (Starrett — "The Eights")
Eight basic movement patterns that define functional human capability. Every client should be able to perform all eight without pain or significant compensation:

1. Squat to depth
2. Hip hinge (deadlift pattern)
3. Lunge
4. Push (horizontal and vertical)
5. Pull (horizontal and vertical)
6. Carry (farmer's carry variations)
7. Rotate
8. Gait (walking and running mechanics)

### Coaching Application
Use this list as a movement screen framework. When a client can't perform one of these without compensation, that's the priority — above any performance goal. A client who can't squat to depth shouldn't be loading a barbell squat.

### Principle: Squat Troubleshooting Framework (Horschig)
The three most common squat limiters:

1. **Ankle mobility restriction** — Most common. Causes heel rise, knee cave, forward lean. Test: can the client drive the knee 5 inches past the toe (wall test) without the heel lifting? If no → ankle mobility work is the first intervention.
2. **Hip socket anatomy** — Some clients physically cannot squat to full depth due to femoral head and acetabulum geometry. This is not a mobility problem — it is anatomy. The solution is stance width modification, not stretching.
3. **Thoracic spine limitation** — Upper back rounding under load. Address with thoracic mobility and bracing work.

### Coaching Application
When a client has a problematic squat: run the ankle mobility test first. If it passes, assess hip anatomy through stance modification. If stance modification doesn't help, assess the thoracic spine. Don't prescribe hip stretching to a client with an ankle restriction.

### Signals
Use when: client reports knee pain during squats, asks about squat depth, shows significant form breakdown.

### Principle: The Load vs. Capacity Model (Horschig)
Pain during training occurs when LOAD exceeds CAPACITY. The solution is not always to remove load — sometimes it's to build capacity.

- Load includes: training volume, intensity, frequency, external stress, sleep deprivation
- Capacity includes: tissue strength and resilience, mobility, stability, recovery status

### Coaching Application
Instead of immediately removing an exercise when a client reports pain, assess which side of the equation to address. Often: a brief deload + specific capacity-building work (single-leg strength, mobility, tempo work) resolves the pain while maintaining training.

### Signals
Use when: client reports joint pain, tendon pain, or movement-related discomfort.

### Principle: Attia's Longevity Framework — The Marginal Decade
Dr. Peter Attia frames every fitness decision around the question: "What do you want your life to look like at 80?" The marginal decade (the last decade of life) is when mobility, strength, and cardiovascular fitness determine whether someone lives independently and with quality. Build toward that decade now.

**Attia's Four Pillars of Longevity Exercise:**

1. **Zone 2 cardio** — 150–180 minutes per week at a conversational pace. Mitochondrial health, metabolic flexibility, cardiovascular efficiency. The most powerful longevity intervention in the exercise domain.
2. **VO2 max training** — Brief high-intensity intervals (2–3x per week) that push the aerobic ceiling. VO2 max is one of the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality.
3. **Strength training** — Preserved muscle mass and strength are the strongest predictors of independence and quality of life in the marginal decade. Progressive resistance training 3–4x per week.
4. **Stability and mobility** — Movement quality, balance, flexibility — the things that determine whether a client falls, and whether they recover from a fall.

### Coaching Application
For clients who frame exercise as vanity or aesthetics: introduce the marginal decade frame. "The choices you make right now determine whether you can pick up your grandchildren. Whether you can carry your groceries. Whether you can travel without limitations at 80." This reframe creates a more durable motivation than appearance goals alone.

### Signals
Use when: client has lost motivation, is over 40, or needs a longer-horizon reason to invest in their health.

### Principle: Daily Steps — The Non-Exercise Activity Variable
10,000 steps per day is a proxy for total non-exercise activity thermogenesis (NEAT). This is distinct from formal exercise. Both matter.

- A client who exercises one hour and sits the remaining 15 hours has a fundamentally different metabolic profile than one who exercises one hour AND accumulates steps throughout the day.
- Every 2,000 additional steps per day is associated with measurable reductions in cardiovascular and all-cause mortality risk.

### Coaching Application
When a client's fat loss has stalled and training is consistent: audit steps before adjusting food or training. A client who averages 3,000 steps/day has a significant lever available that doesn't require more gym time.

### Signals
Use when: fat loss plateau with consistent training, client has a sedentary job or lifestyle, or as a low-barrier first intervention.
